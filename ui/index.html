On-chain guardrails

Every strategy decision is checked by the vault before execution.

  • ✓ Conservation —

    Total assets may not fall across a rebalance. Capital can move between venues; it cannot leave. A plan that would shrink the vault is rejected outright.

  • ✓ Venue cap —

    The largest share any single venue may hold. Shown as the highest cap across active venues — no plan can concentrate the book past it, however confident the strategy is.

  • ✓ Turnover —

    The maximum fraction of the book that may be rotated in one rebalance. This bounds churn, so an agent cannot grind the vault down through repeated large reshuffles.

  • ✓ FTSO band —

    The reference price in the plan must sit within this band of Flare's FTSO XRP/USD feed. A price the market never printed cannot be used to justify an allocation.

  • ✓ Signal freshness —

    The attested market observation behind a plan must be newer than this. Stale data cannot be replayed to re-run a decision the market has already moved past.

Enclave identity —

Hardware attestation quotes are simulated on Flare's testnet — which is exactly why these five checks are enforced on-chain instead of trusting the enclave.

How it holds together

  1. 01 Private strategy The allocation model runs inside an enclave and never leaves it.
  2. 02 Public inputs The market data it reads is attested on-chain by ~100 providers.
  3. 03 On-chain guardrails Five checks bound every plan before the vault will execute it.
  4. 04 User funds Capital moves between venues. It cannot be moved out.

A malicious enclave can still pick a poor-but-legal allocation, or refuse to act. It cannot move funds out, concentrate, churn, or act on a price the market never printed. The enclave controls strategy quality, never fund safety.
